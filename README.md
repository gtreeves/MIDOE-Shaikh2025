# Abstract

The TGF-β/Smad signal transduction pathway regulates growth, development, and homeostasis of tissues and is conserved at a molecular and functional level from worms to humans. The pathway is activated when transforming growth factor - β (TGF-β), a ligand from the super-family of TGF-β, binds to the cognate transmembrane receptors resulting in the phosphorylation of Smad2. The activated PSmad2 undergoes a series of biochemical reactions with transcriptional activator Smad4 to form several homomeric and heteromeric oligomers, including the transcription factor (PSmad2)<sub> 2 </sub>/Smad4 which regulates target gene expression. Quantitative approaches such as fluorescence microscopy, live cell imaging and mathematical modeling have been used to estimate the dynamics of (PSmad2)<sub> 2 </sub>/Smad4. However, due to the emergent dynamics of Smad2-Smad4 interactions, deconvolving the dynamics of the (PSmad2)<sub> 2 </sub>/Smad4 is challenging. To overcome this, we employed profile log-likelihood to inspire maximally informed design of experiments (MIDOE) to infer the dynamics of (PSmad2)<sub> 2 </sub>/Smad4. We show that the Smad model is sloppy, and as such it has large uncertainties associated with the parameters (Ο~10<sup> 15 </sup>) and the eigenvalues of the Fisher Information Matrix (FIM) span over several decades; resulting in the best-fit parameters generating highly under-constrained predictions. To constrain the Smad model predictions, we employed profile log-likelihood (PLL) analysis to determine the range over which each parameter cannot be learned from data — parametric and structural unidentifiabilities; and design experiments to maximally inform parameter estimation. We generated these experiments computationally and repeated this process to ultimately constrain the dynamics of (PSmad2)2/Smad4.
